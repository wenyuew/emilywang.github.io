Who is honored, and what they did

The 14 Class-A war criminals were convicted by the Tokyo Tribunal (1946–48). All 14 are enshrined at Yasukuni. The seven who were executed are also honored at Kōa Kannon and Mount Sangane.

Class B and C: examples of executed convicts

About 920 Class B and C convicts were executed across Asia. Yasukuni enshrined 984 of them between 1959 and 1967 without asking their families. The shrine doesn't publish its list, so no single name below is individually confirmed.

How to spot these places on your trip

  • Skip any shrine named 護国神社 / 護國神社 (Gokoku Jinja) or 招魂社 (Shōkonsha, the older name).
  • 忠魂碑 (chūkonhi) stones honor a town's war dead in general. They sometimes stand inside ordinary shrines.
  • Famous sites like Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, Sensō-ji, Meiji Jingu, Tōdai-ji, Ise Jingu and Itsukushima have no link to the war dead.
  • Chidorigafuchi National Cemetery in Tokyo holds unidentified war dead. It enshrines no one and honors no war criminals.
Pin locations are approximate, and the map outline is simplified. Gokoku shrine pins outside major cities are placed at the city, not the exact shrine. Tokyo's is Yasukuni itself, and Kanagawa has none. Sources: Nippon.com on the enshrinement of war criminals, Takenaka, Asia-Pacific Journal, Gokoku shrines (Wikipedia), Kōyasan Okunoin memorial.
